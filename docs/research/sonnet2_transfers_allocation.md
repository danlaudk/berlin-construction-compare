**Key structural fact, VERIFIED from the 9th report's own text:** the collapse after 2021 is not a policy choice but a legal one. The Bundesverwaltungsgericht ruling of 9 November 2021 (BVerwG 4 C 1.20) held that Vorkaufsrecht may only be exercised to avert a concretely specified future violation of the Erhaltungsverordnung's purpose — not generally to prevent price-driven displacement — which "in fast allen relevanten Grundstücksverkäufen" since then makes exercise legally impossible from the outset ("Negativzeugnis"). The Senate's own report calls the instrument "seit der Entscheidung des Bundesverwaltungsgerichts faktisch fast nicht mehr nutzbar" and calls for a federal Baugesetzbuch amendment, which has not happened. Also VERIFIED: number of soziale Erhaltungsgebiete rose from 81 to 82 in 2025 (new: "Stresow," Spandau).

**Reading.** Even in the pre-2021 peak year, Vorkaufsrecht-plus-Abwendung secured on the order of 2,000–4,000 flats/year city-wide — already a small fraction of LWU purchase volume, let alone new construction. Since the 2021 ruling, the mechanism delivers a handful of cases and 50–75 flats per year. This lever is now functionally dead pending federal legislation that successive Senates have not obtained.

**The single biggest annual transfer in the whole 2011–2025 window is the 2021/22 Vonovia/Deutsche Wohnen sale (14,750 flats, landing mostly in the 2022 Ankauf figure).** Outside that one transaction, annual transfer volume into public/non-profit hands runs at roughly 500–5,000 flats/year — an order of magnitude below the >200,000 figure debated for full-scale Vergesellschaftung, and also below the ~11,000 flats/year of new construction. ### 1f. People newly housed by these transfers: **zero** (by construction), and the occupancy evidence

- **Vonovia/Deutsche Wohnen Berlin portfolio vacancy at the time of the 2021 sale: ~0.5–0.8%** (deutsche-wohnen.com states Berlin vacancy of 0.7%; Vonovia's own reporting and DZ Bank estimates put it at 0.5–0.8%; "apartments in Berlin are almost completely occupied" per Berliner Zeitung reporting on Vonovia rents). SECONDARY (WebSearch synthesis of deutsche-wohnen.com's own "Fakten" page and press coverage), but directionally unambiguous and consistent with the general corporate-portfolio vacancy figures elsewhere in this research effort (BBU-member vacancy 1.6% end-2024, cooperatives alone 0.8% — see §3).
- Implication: at ≥99% occupancy, essentially all 14,750 flats already had tenants living in them at the moment of sale. The transaction changed the landlord of record, not the number of households housed. The same logic applies to Vorkaufsrecht/Abwendung cases by legal construction — the entire purpose of the Vorkaufsrecht mechanism is to intervene in a sale of an occupied, tenanted building in a soziales Erhaltungsgebiet to prevent displacement of the existing tenants, so by definition these are not vacant units being filled.
- **Conclusion: the number of people newly housed by LWU purchases, the 2021 Vonovia/DW sale, and Vorkaufsrecht/Abwendungsvereinbarung combined is zero**, because in every documented case the flats were already occupied before the transfer. This mirrors — and independently corroborates from the transactional/occupancy side — the DIW aktuell Nr. 126 finding (already verified by sonnetB) that "die Vergesellschaftung schafft keinen zusätzlichen Wohnraum."

**This is the primary-source confirmation of Bernt & Holm's "9,349/yr average 2016–2021" figure** (already cited as done in the task brief): my VERIFIED 2021 figure of 9,622 and 2022 figure of 9,348 bracket their average almost exactly, and the broader 2016–2024 range (8,189–9,622 WBS lettings/year) is consistent with — and narrows — their headline number using the Senate's own primary reports rather than a secondary citation.

**Important scope caveat (do not conflate with the 7,000/yr Bernt & Holm transfer figure):** these are re-lettings of *already-existing, already-occupied* LWU flats to a new WBS-eligible tenant when the old tenant moves out (turnover/Fluktuation, ~3.3–4.4% of stock per company per year per koop_bericht_2024.pdf §2.5). This is a *reallocation among tenants*, not a transfer of previously private flats into public ownership, and not a net addition of housing. It is the appropriate "allocation baseline" lever — i.e., how LWU manage Vergabe within their existing stock — as distinct from the ownership-transfer levers in §1.

### 2b. Declining denominator — fewer flats are coming up for re-letting at all

A separate, important trend VERIFIED from koop_bericht_2024.pdf (§2.5, lines 366–376): total Bestandswohnungen-Wiedervermietungen fell from 13,471 (2023) to 12,398 (2024), "nochmals um rund 8%" — continuing a multi-year decline ("Seit mehreren Jahren ist die Anzahl der zur Wiedervermietung kommenden Bestandswohnungen konstant rückläufig"). Falling Fluktuation (turnover) — the same mechanism the bank-commissioned empirica/Simons study (already verified by sonnetB, ~13% Fluktuation drop under full Vergesellschaftung) flags as a risk of socialization — is **already happening today**, independent of any Vergesellschaftung, and is shrinking the pool available for WBS allocation year over year. This directly bears on the "allocation" lever's ceiling: even a 100%-WBS quota cannot help if the number of flats turning over keeps falling.

### 2c. The lever card at 63% vs. 75%

The Kooperationsvereinbarung's WBS re-letting quota target is **63%** (since the April 2021 Ergänzungsvereinbarung; raised again to varying graduated sub-quotas for WBS-100%/WBS-140%/WBS-220% income bands in the 2024 KoopV, see koop_bericht_2024.pdf §"gestaffelte Wiedervermietungsquoten"). The task brief flags a "63% vs. 75%" discrepancy to resolve against — note the campaign's draft Vergesellschaftung law proposes a 75% WBS-letting quota for the *new* AöR's stock specifically, not a retroactive change to the existing LWU KoopV quota; the two numbers apply to different, non-overlapping stocks (LWU ~365,134 units at 63% vs. a hypothetical AöR of up to ~240,000 units at 75%), so they are not directly substitutable targets for the same pool.

Using the VERIFIED 2024 base (12,398 total re-lettings; 8,189 at 63%-plus achieved 66.1%):

- **At the actual 63% KoopV target, applied to the 2016–2024 VERIFIED average re-letting volume (~13,500/yr):** ≈ 13,500 × 0.63 ≈ **8,505/yr** WBS households served (INFERRED, close to Bernt & Holm's 7,000/yr and the directly observed 8,189–9,622 range).
- **At a hypothetical 75% quota applied to the same ~13,500/yr re-letting volume:** ≈ 13,500 × 0.75 ≈ **10,125/yr** (INFERRED) — i.e. raising the quota from 63% to 75% on the *existing* LWU re-letting flow would add roughly **1,600–1,900 more WBS households/year** (INFERRED, not an official Senate estimate), not the 220,000-dwelling scale the expropriation debate operates at. This is the correct "lever" framing: quota increases act on the *existing turnover flow* (~12,000–15,000/yr), not on the *stock* (~365,000 LWU units or ~220,000 debated AöR units).
- Caveat: the task brief's own worked numbers (6,999 vs. ~8,325/yr) imply a smaller base re-letting volume (~11,100/yr) than the VERIFIED 12,398–15,385/yr range found here; I did not reconcile which base year the brief's arithmetic assumed. Flag for the synthesizer: **use the directly observed per-year WBS-letting counts above (8,189–9,622) rather than re-deriving them from an assumed base**, since primary-source annual figures are now available and are more reliable than a recomputed share.

## Summary for the synthesizer

**Per-year transfer table (§1e):** transfer volume into public/non-profit hands is lumpy and small except for one event. VERIFIED LWU Ankauf: 3,067 (2021) → **11,644 (2022, driven almost entirely by the 14,750-flat Vonovia/Deutsche Wohnen sale)** → 512 (2023) → 238 (2024) → 4,609 (2025). Vorkaufsrecht/Abwendung adds only 22–2,350 flats/year on top, now collapsed to ~50–75/year since the 2021 BVerwG ruling made the instrument largely unusable. **People newly housed by any of this: zero**, because Vonovia/Deutsche Wohnen Berlin portfolio vacancy was ~0.5–0.8% at the time of sale (near-full occupancy) and Vorkaufsrecht/Abwendung by legal design only ever applies to occupied buildings being sold.
