Research date: 2026-11-15. Tags: **VERIFIED** means I opened the primary source and read the figure. **SNIPPET** means the figure came from a search-engine summary or a secondary report and was not checked against the primary source. **INFERRED** means my own arithmetic or memory. **Reading.** Market-active vacancy of 0.3% of about 1.9 m flats in multi-family buildings is roughly 5,000–6,000 flats (INFERRED). That is far below any functional reserve. The census figure of about 40,700 includes flats under renovation, awaiting demolition, between tenants, and second homes not reported. Only the long-term part (11,764) is a plausible activation target, and much of it has structural reasons.

**Adds / transfers / frees.** Activation adds no new squares. It frees existing units. A realistic one-off ceiling is a fraction of the 11,764 long-term vacant, so about 3–6 squares at most (INFERRED).

**Zweckentfremdungsverbot (ban on misuse since 1.5.2014).**
- Cumulative dwellings returned to the housing market: 26,550 by Dec 2023 and 27,813 by Dec 2024 (SNIPPET, Tagesspiegel "Knapp 30.000 Fälle seit 2014", based on a Schriftliche Anfrage). Implied rate 2024: about 1,260 per year. This cumulative figure includes holiday flats, vacancy, commercial use and demolition cases, and some are counted per proceeding, not per unique flat. Split by category not found in this pass.
- Registration numbers for holiday flats: "over 60,000 issued 2018–2025" (SNIPPET, low-quality blog source; likely conflates applications and numbers). Treat as unverified.
- Drucksache 19/24712, Schriftliche Anfrage Katrin Schmidberger (Grüne), 5.1.2026, answer 21.1.2026, "Ferienwohnungen (FeWos) und Zweckentfremdung" – **VERIFIED**. It contains per-district counts of illegal holiday flats returned to housing, 2022–2025. Examples: Tempelhof-Schöneberg 50/26/66/60; Neukölln 85/128/46/25; Mitte (Mitte+Tiergarten+Wedding) 52/60/135/84; Treptow-Köpenick 20/14/9/1; Steglitz-Zehlendorf 17/12/11/26; Reinickendorf 11/9/18/2; Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf 2/2/0/0 (orders); Marzahn-Hellersdorf 0. Pankow, Lichtenberg, Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg and Spandau could not report. Summed over reporting districts, holiday-flat returns run at about 200–300 per year (INFERRED from the table). After the OVG ruling (OVG 5 B 5/22) Mitte reported 125 flats returned, Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf 25, Tempelhof-Schöneberg 170 of 360 proceedings closed. The Drucksache is the best per-Bezirk source but is incomplete and inconsistent between districts.

**Counter-argument (proponent).** Census vacancy of 40,700 equals about 3.7 years of completions at the 2025 rate. Proponents also argue enforcement is understaffed. The data reply: market-active vacancy is 0.3%, and the census figure is mostly frictional.

**Dataset.** Inside Airbnb (insideairbnb.com/get-the-data) publishes quarterly Berlin snapshots: listings.csv(.gz) with about 75 variables per listing (id, host_id, neighbourhood_cleansed = Ortsteil, neighbourhood_group = Bezirk, room_type, availability_365, number_of_reviews, license field holding the registration number, lat/lon), plus calendar and reviews files. **Reading.** Even if every entire-home listing were a permanent flat taken off the market and all were returned, that is about 6 squares. Many listings are registered, part-time or not real dwellings. Realistic yield is 1–3 squares one-off. It frees, it does not add.

**Reading.** Expropriation, the 2021 purchase and Vorkaufsrecht are all pure transfers. The 220,000 flats would be about 220 squares changing colour, with zero new squares. Even in 2025, about half of LWU stock growth (4,609 of 9,311) came from purchases, which are also transfers. I found no official body that claims expropriation increases supply. The Expertenkommission addressed legality and compensation, not supply.

**Counter-argument (proponent).** DWE argues socialised stock lowers rents, stops displacement, and that freed public capital or lower returns enable construction. A related claim is that LWUs build more than private landlords per flat owned. Neither changes the count of dwellings. Compensation of €29–39 bn would equal the construction cost of very roughly 70,000–100,000 new flats at €350–400k each (INFERRED, sensitive to assumptions).

## (c) Which levers are not marginal

- **Expropriation is large but is a transfer.** At about 220 squares it dwarfs everything, so the visualisation must show it as recoloured squares, not as filled gaps. Hostile readers will say it was never meant to add supply. That is fair and should be stated.
- **Attic, Aufstockung and office conversion are not trivial.** Combined theoretical potential of about 30,000–65,000 is 30–65 squares. But these are construction, permitted and counted in F II 1/F II 2. They belong on the "build" side, not as an alternative to it.
- **Vacancy and holiday flats are marginal.** Realistic yield is in single-digit squares even under generous assumptions. The census 40,700 figure will be used against you. Show it, then show that 0.3% is market-active.
- **Overhang is the nearest-term non-marginal lever.** About 48 squares already permitted. Getting them built is new construction, which strengthens the core argument.
- **The demand side is big enough to absorb everything.** Need of 211,000 plus about 20–30 squares of homeless households versus about 11 squares built per year.
